# Diabetes mellitus overview

Diabetes mellitus is a group of metabolic disorders characterised by high blood glucose. Type 1 diabetes results from autoimmune destruction of pancreatic beta cells. Type 2 diabetes results from insulin resistance with a progressive loss of insulin secretion.

Diabetes can be diagnosed with an HbA1c of 6.5% or higher, a fasting plasma glucose of 126 mg/dL (7.0 mmol/L) or higher, or a 2-hour plasma glucose of 200 mg/dL (11.1 mmol/L) or higher during an oral glucose tolerance test. Without symptoms, an abnormal result should be confirmed by repeat testing.

Long-term complications include retinopathy, nephropathy, neuropathy and cardiovascular disease. Regular screening for these complications is part of routine diabetes care.

Insulin and sulfonylureas lower blood glucose effectively but can cause hypoglycaemia, especially when meals are missed or physical activity increases. Patient education on recognising low blood glucose is an important part of treatment.
