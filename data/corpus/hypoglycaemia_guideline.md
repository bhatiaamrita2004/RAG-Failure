# Hypoglycaemia management in adults

Hypoglycaemia is classified by blood glucose level. Level 1 hypoglycaemia is a blood glucose below 70 mg/dL (3.9 mmol/L) and at or above 54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L). Level 2 hypoglycaemia is a blood glucose below 54 mg/dL (3.0 mmol/L). Level 3 hypoglycaemia is a severe event with altered mental or physical status that needs assistance from another person.

For a conscious adult who can swallow safely, give 15 to 20 g of fast-acting glucose, such as glucose tablets. Recheck blood glucose after 15 minutes. If blood glucose is still low, repeat the treatment. Once blood glucose returns to normal, the person should eat a meal or snack to prevent recurrence.

If the person cannot swallow safely or is unconscious, do not give food or drink by mouth. Give glucagon, for example as an injection or nasal glucagon, and call emergency services. Family members and carers of people at risk of severe hypoglycaemia should be trained to use glucagon.

People who have repeated or unexplained hypoglycaemia should have their glucose-lowering treatment reviewed, including the dose and timing of insulin or sulfonylureas.
