# Emergency treatment of anaphylaxis

Anaphylaxis is a severe, potentially life-threatening systemic allergic reaction. Recognise it by rapid onset of airway, breathing or circulation problems, often with skin or mucosal changes such as hives or swelling.

Epinephrine (adrenaline) is the first-line treatment for anaphylaxis and should be given without delay. Give epinephrine by intramuscular injection into the anterolateral mid-thigh. The dose is 0.01 mg/kg of the 1 mg/mL solution, up to a maximum single dose of 0.5 mg in adults. The intramuscular dose can be repeated every 5 to 15 minutes if symptoms do not improve.

Place the patient lying down with legs raised, or sitting up if breathing is difficult. Give high-flow oxygen and intravenous fluids when needed. Antihistamines and corticosteroids are adjuncts only and must not delay or replace epinephrine.

Observe patients after recovery because symptoms can return, and refer them for allergy assessment. Patients at risk should carry an epinephrine auto-injector and be trained to use it.
